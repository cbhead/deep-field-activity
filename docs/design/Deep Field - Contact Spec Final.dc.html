Deep Field Contact spec · final · 31 Aug 2026

The six contacts, specified

No options in this document. Every shape, every value and every state is decided, in units that can be typed straight into contactShape.ts and theme.ts.

1 · The one rendering rule

Contacts were baked neutral and GPU-tinted per type, because one texture for the whole roster was worth a lot. That is no longer what is happening: six distinct silhouettes already mean six bakes, so the tint is buying nothing — and in the first draft of this spec it was paid for by dropping the Mote's and the Cluster's highlights, which is the wrong trade. So:

Each contact bakes in its own final colours, and every internal value is derived from its token by a lightness multiplier. No tint, no alpha tricks: a highlight is a multiplier above 1, a darker step one below. A reskin is still one line in theme.enemies, because the whole ramp is computed from that single value at bake time.

Three consequences:

  • The token stays the class hue. The Bulwark keeps #c9a6ae; its lit apex #e3c3c9 is that value at ×1.13 and its deepest wedge #5f4148 at ×0.47. Nothing in the palette table moves to accommodate the renderer.
  • The approved highlights are kept. The Mote's near-white core #fff1ec and the Cluster's #ffc9c2 nuclei are ×1.35 and ×1.22 of their tokens. They are the pop that makes M1 and the trefoil read at 12px.
  • Mocks and bakes now use the same numbers. Every value is a resolved colour rather than an alpha over an unknown ground, so a specimen here and the texture the game bakes cannot disagree — a class of error the alpha rule quietly allowed.
  • The hit flash changes mechanism, and it has to. tint multiplies, so lerping toward fx.hitFlash (0xffffff) brightens a neutral bake and is a no-op against a coloured one. The flash becomes an additive re-draw of the contact's own texture at lerped alpha — no new texture, one extra sprite only while a contact is actually flashing, and additive can exceed the source, which is what keeps it the top of the contrast hierarchy. fx.hitFlash stays as the additive colour.

Geometry conventions

All radii below are fractions of R, the contact's own body radius (def.radius × TILE_PX) — the same quantity worldView scales the sprite by, so every shape holds across the roster's 2.7× size range. Angles are degrees clockwise from three o'clock, as COLLAR_SECTORS uses. All six bake into the same square frame fixed by the existing halo circle at glowRatio 1.75, so nothing about worldView's scaling changes.

None, and that is the point — the Drifter is the shape every other contact is a deviation from. It gets the plainest form available so the others have something to be read against.

Damaged · slowed · slow field · hit flash · dying. The only rotated contact: a comet that does not point along travel is meaningless. Tail length is capped at 1.45R so it fits the existing bake frame.

Monolith

bodydisc 1.00Rtoken, ×1.0

Derivation

The only contact with a fully opaque body, because it is the only one that is nothing but hull. Strata and core sit darker than the shell so it reads as dense rather than as hollow — the inverse of the Drifter's translucent mantle.

States

Damaged · slowed · slow field · hit flash · dying. Slowest in the roster, so it is the contact most often in a slow field.

Warden

Protected

Derivation

The inner rim is the overshield's shape, read as a layer inside the hull — but drawn in the contact's own tint, so it never competes with the blue band that reports the shield's actual level.

States

The only contact that can hold every state at once: damaged, shielded, shield broken, regenerating, slowed, in a slow field, flashing. Its hull bar is always drawn once the shield is below full — that is deliberate and stays.

Lobe count is splitInto.count. A Cluster that split into four would draw four lobes with no other edit. The nuclei make the children countable, which is the whole reason this contact needs a silhouette instead of an interior — it must be identifiable before the killing shot, not after.

Damaged · slowed · slow field · hit flash · dying · split death signature (§3.9). Not rotated: it is a cluster, not a vehicle, and a tumbling trefoil reads as debris.

Derivation

The hexagon still means plated — it remains the only non-round contact. The six wedges give it a machined read no other contact has, and the seam width is derived from armor: a contact with armour 2 gets a visibly thinner plate line. The lit apex fixes a single light source at the top.

States

Damaged · slowed · slow field · hit flash · dying. No shield. Armour is permanent, so it is carried by the silhouette and never by transient chrome.

3 · States

Every state below is drawn by creepChrome.ts or worldView.ts, not baked. The governing rule stands and is not relaxed by anything here: draw nothing for a contact that has nothing to report. Offsets are measured out from R, as the current code already does.

Untouched
Sprite only. No bar, no ring, no halo.
Damaged
Hull bar appears at R+6. Fixed 32px track — a fraction is only readable against a common width.
Critical
Below 30% the fill goes fx.hpLow. Unchanged.
Slowed
Clock at R+3. Width 1.5 + bite×5; arc burns down clockwise from twelve.
Shielded
Band above the hull bar, never a ring. Bar is drawn even at full hull.
Shield broken
Band empties; shieldBroke fires once at the point of the hit.
Regenerating
Filled halo at R+3 while the band refills. A fill, where the slow is a stroke.
Hit flash
The contact's own texture, drawn over itself in additive blend at lerped alpha. Remains the brightest event on the board.
The Drifter carries states 1–4 and the Warden 5–7, because those are the contacts that can hold them. The flash applies to all six.

Slow field — three or more held together

Twelve held contacts in a file: one soft field behind the group at 0.07 alpha, and every contact keeps its own clock on top. The field says "this mass is held"; the clocks say which one is about to speed up. Threshold is 3, reach 1.15 tiles, pad 0.62 tiles — as shipped.

Split death — Cluster only

0 ms · about to die
~120 ms · three pips out
~250 ms · three Motes walking
Three pips in the child's tint, fired outward from the kill point over ~250ms, keyed on splitInto in the creepKilled handler. This is the payoff for the coral lineage: the parent is red, the pips are coral, and the Motes that appear are the same coral — so the causal chain is visible in one continuous read.

4 · What changes in code

FileChange
contactShape.ts
new		Pure geometry in units of R, no colour, no Pixi, no DOM — the stationShape.ts precedent. Discs, lenses, polygons and wedges, each carrying a lightness multiplier (and an alpha where the shape is meant to be translucent). Both renderers read it; neither imports the other.
textures.tsdrawCreep becomes one bake per contact type, from contactShape, in final colour — each internal value a lightness multiple of the type's token. Contacts no longer take a tint; stations and projectiles keep BAKE_NEUTRAL and theirs. Frame still fixed by the halo circle, so worldView's def.radius / CREEP_BAKE_RADIUS scaling is untouched.
icons.tscontactIcon reads the same geometry and emits the same resolved ramp — it already imports THEME, so it calls the same step(token, k) the bake does. currentColor is gone: a single colour cannot express a six-wedge Bulwark or a Mote with a near-white core, and a legend that renders a flat silhouette while the board renders a ramp is exactly the drift stationShape.ts exists to prevent. The size-compression band stays — it preserves order across a 2.7× radius span and keeps the smallest readable.
theme.tsSix values in enemies, plus a small step(token, k) helper. step is shared by both consumers — the bake and contactIcon — for the same reason the geometry is: two copies of a ramp is two ramps that drift. No second table of hexes; every internal value derives from the one token.
worldView.tsRotation for the Mote only, from the route tangent; everything else keeps rotation = 0. The tint-lerp flash is removed — contacts are no longer tinted, so it would do nothing.
creepChrome.ts
flash		The flash moves here: a second sprite of the contact's own texture, blendMode: 'add', alpha lerped down over the flash window. Suppressed like everything else on this layer — a contact that is not being struck draws nothing.
creepChrome.tsOne addition: the split pips, with their own suppression (splitInto !== null only). Bars, clocks, fields and halos are unchanged.
legend.tsNothing structural — it already calls contactIcon and derives its copy from the def. But drop the .c-<id> colour hook it and hud.ts set today: contact glyphs now carry their own resolved values, so that class no longer drives anything and will otherwise sit there looking load-bearing.

The one budget that moves. The contact layer goes from two baked textures to six, and contacts stop being tinted. The property that mattered was never "exactly two" or "tinted" — it was not one per entity, and six textures for 250 contacts keeps that completely. Do not add a seventh bake for a state; states are chrome.
