Deep Field Build spec v1 · 30 Aug 2026

Every surface, before and after

The companion to the design review: sixteen surfaces, each shown as it renders today beside what it should render, with the reason the change is worth making and the property it must not break.

Read the review first for why these are the priorities. This document is the what — a thing to build from and to check the build against. Every number quoted is live from src/content/; nothing here changes balance, only what the player can see.

How each entry is laid out

BeforeWhat ships today, drawn faithfully — including the parts that work.
AfterThe proposal, at the size and value it would actually render at.
ProblemWhat the current state costs the player, stated as a failure, not a taste.
ChangeThe smallest edit that fixes it, named in the terms the code uses.
Must holdThe existing property the change is not allowed to trade away. If a build breaks one of these, it is wrong even if it looks better.

1 · The board

Six entries. All of it lives in mapLayer.ts, textures.ts and theme.ts; none of it touches the simulation.

1.1  The field

Ground, grid and starfield — everything under the route.

Before
Two ground tints four values apart at 0.72 alpha, a 1px grid at even weight to the board edge, 300 static stars. Flat from corner to corner.
After
A board-wide gradient lit from the pulsar at ~0.14 in #b5abfc, a second dimmer star pass for depth, and the grid masked away toward the edges.
Problem

390 identical tiles with a uniform grid read as graph paper. Nothing tells the eye where the board's centre of gravity is, so every corner looks equally important — including the ones with no route in them.

Change

One Graphics gradient node under the tile field, one extra seeded star pass, and a radial mask on the grid stroke. Three additions, all baked once, zero per-frame cost.

Must hold

Placement legibility near stations. The grid may fade at the far edges; it may not fade anywhere a player builds. And the starfield stays static — motion in the background means "a contact".

1.2  The route corridor

43 tiles, six turns, spawn at left to pulsar at right.

Before
One flat fill (#1b1f36), pathEdge: null, square corners. Legible, and silent about everything else.
After
Fill ramps along route distance toward the pulsar, a 2px hot centre line mitred at corners, and an outward spill that lights the tiles beside the road.
Problem

A flat corridor says where contacts walk and nothing else — not which way they travel, not how close to the core they are. The most information-dense object on the board carries one bit.

Change

Per-tile fill interpolated on route distance (the waypoint list already gives it), one Graphics polyline for the centre line, one blurred strip texture for spill. Still built once.

Must hold

Greyscale legibility — the route reads lighter than the field by value, never by hue alone. And the route may glow; it may never out-glow a station.

1.3  Stream motion

The one moving part of the board layer.

Before
Static. Correct, and slightly inert — a current that does not move is a road.
After
≤ 0.10 α · 0.35 tiles/s
One TilingSprite per straight run (seven on Switchback), scrolling toward the pulsar. Corners take a static cap so no run has to turn.
Problem

Nothing — this is the one entry that adds a cost rather than removing one. It is here because a static stream undersells the board, and because the cheap way to do it must be written down before someone does it the expensive way.

Change

Seven tilePosition.x writes per frame, one shared texture, one batch. Speed is constant and does not scale with the 1×/2×/4× multiplier. Prefs toggle beside reach circles; off by default under prefers-reduced-motion.

Must hold

At 4× on Switchback, a single Mote is still the first thing the eye finds. If it is not, halve the alpha before touching the speed — a dim fast crawl reads as current, a bright slow one reads as an object.

1.4  Nebula scenery

Blocked tiles — the thing you cannot build on.

Before
Puffs inscribed inside each 40px tile. A disc meets its tile edge at one point, so a clump leaves a dark cross tracing the grid — four coins, not one cloud.
After
One seeded blob field in board space, drawn as a single node under the tiles. Blocked tiles keep a faint plate outline so "you cannot build here" is still tile-accurate.
Problem

The cloud tiles, and a tiling artefact in a starfield is louder than the seam it was avoiding. Scenery that traces the grid tells the player the board is made of squares at the exact moment it should be telling them it is made of gas.

Change

Move the puffs from the tile bake into one board-space Graphics generated from the blocked-tile set, seeded like the starfield so it never reshuffles between reloads.

Must hold

Blocked tiles must stay readable as tiles — the cloud is atmosphere, the plate is the rule. And the nebula stays below a station in the contrast order.

1.5  Pulsar and spawn

The thing you are defending, and the thing you are defending it from.

Before
Both drawn inside one 40px tile. The core is the game's objective and it is smaller than a station.
After
Pulsar grown to ~2.5 tiles of falling-alpha rings under a bloomed core. The spawn chevron gets the same treatment at a quarter of the intensity: arrival is quiet, the core is loud.
Problem

The player's whole loss condition is a 40px detail at the board edge. When a leak happens the eye has nowhere to have already been looking.

Change

Scale PULSAR_RINGS off the tile and onto a ~2.5-tile radius, add a bloom shoulder under the core, and give the spawn a matching low-intensity halo in #f9a8d4.

Must hold

The pulsar is the brightest structural object and still dimmer than a contact taking a hit. It also cannot eat buildable ground — the halo is drawn under the tiles, not over them.

1.6  Three sector fields

Same route grammar, three grounds. Foreground fixed campaign-wide.

01 · Switchback
Indigo deep field. The shipped baseline, finished.
02 · Cascade
Steel-cyan, thin nebula, harder stars. The open board should feel emptier.
03 · Pincer
Cold teal-black, densest nebula pressing both lanes.
Problem

Three boards look like one board. The campaign's only sense of travel is the level name in the top bar.

Change

board becomes SECTOR_FIELDS keyed by id; LevelDef gains field — an id, not a colour, so content keeps its no-presentation rule. Textures bake per field at level load.

Must hold

The same Lance is the same blue and the same Drifter the same pink on every board. And no warm field, at any value — warm belongs to the contacts. Re-verify the contrast budget once per field, not once in total.

2.5  Collapsed deck

Before
Wave 7 running — 6 of 24 alive Deck ▲ Tab
A live status strip repeating what the Incoming ribbon says forty pixels above it, in the same words — and doing it in space that belongs to the board.
After
Deck ▲ Tab
The handle alone, bottom-right. DECK_PX becomes state-dependent, so a closed deck gives the board back most of 150px.
Problem

Two live readouts of one fact is one readout and one distraction — and the duplicate is the one sitting where the play area should be. It also rebuilds on every kill, because deckKey folds the alive count in whenever the deck is closed.

Change

Delete renderStripStatus. Move the crit signal into the ribbon (fill goes --danger at ≤5 lives). The closed deck's key becomes a constant and stops touching the DOM entirely.

Must hold

The crit signal must survive the cut — it is the strip's one non-duplicate. And fitCanvas has to letterbox board and deck as one unit at both deck heights, so the board must not jump distractingly on Tab.

Before

Works. Lives crit is a red numeral; the ribbon fill is accent whatever is happening.
After

At ≤5 lives the whole ribbon goes danger — fill, border and a soft rim — so the alarm is the width of the screen rather than one numeral. Three contact dots replace the word "alive"; the $ goes.
Problem

The ribbon has to absorb the deleted strip's crit signal, and a single red digit is a weak alarm on a screen where the player is looking at the board. It also has to carry the strip's job without gaining the strip's word count.

Change

Danger state on the ribbon container, not just the Lives cell. Dots for inbound count. Drop the currency sigil now that costs elsewhere have dropped it too.

Must hold

The ribbon's fill is still a progress value — the danger tint must not make it read as a second quantity. Verify side by side before shipping: if it reads as "42% doomed", use a rim and border only and leave the fill accent.

3 · Status effects

The channel budget, decided once: shape for permanent properties, bar-adjacent for pools, one ring for one timed debuff, emitter-side for anything about the tower's own state.

3.1  Slow, in a crowd

Before
One cyan ring per held contact. A slow's whole job is to bunch contacts into a file, so the effect manufactures the exact density at which its own indicator stops working.
After
merge at ≥3
Rings merge: at three or more overlapping, draw one soft field enclosing the group instead of N outlines. One contact keeps its ring — the singular case is where a ring reads best.
Problem

Ring soup. Six outlines at 40px spacing become a texture, and the player loses both "which are slowed" and "how many are there" at once.

Change

In effects.ts, cluster slowed contacts by proximity per frame and draw one capsule field per cluster of ≥3. Same colour, same layer, fewer marks.

Must hold

The ring stays the only ring on a contact. Shield is a band, armour is a silhouette — nothing else may claim this channel, or the merge stops meaning "slowed".

3.2  Filament ramp

Before
cold and charged look identical
Nothing is drawn. A Filament three seconds into a Monolith is at ×3 damage and looks exactly like one that just switched target and lost everything.
After
spin-up 20% · at ceiling
Charge drawn on the emitter, as a ring that fills clockwise to the ceiling and visibly snaps back to zero on target switch. Core brightens with it. Nothing is added to the target.
Problem

The mechanic whose entire value is time-on-target has no representation, so the player cannot see that a retarget just cost them a ×3 multiplier — and therefore cannot learn to pair it with a slow.

Change

A per-tower charge arc in towerChrome.ts, driven by focusTime against rampMax / rampPerSecond. The snap-back on reset is the important frame — do not tween it.

Must hold

The collar's three upgrade sectors own fixed angles. The charge ring must sit at a different radius, not steal a sector, or the two positional codes collide.

3.3  Cluster split, and shield regen

Before
−44
a Cluster death looks like any death · regen start is silent
Left: the kill that makes things worse is indistinguishable from the kill that makes things better. Right: the shield band is correct, but nothing marks the moment regen begins.
After
three pips outward · regen halo while filling
Left: a Cluster's death fires three Mote-tinted pips outward from the kill point — the split is announced before the children walk. Right: the band fills visibly and the contact wears a faint blue halo while regen is running.
Problem

Two mechanics that punish the player fire silently. A Cluster killed at the front of the route is a mistake the player only learns about three Motes later, and a Warden that healed in a coverage gap arrives whole with no prior warning.

Change

A death signature keyed on splitInto in the creepKilled handler — three pips in the child's tint, ~250ms. A halo on any contact whose shieldTimer has expired and whose shield is below max.

Must hold

Both are transient and must not compete with the hit flash, which stays the brightest event on the board. The regen halo is blue and must not read as the cyan slow field.

4 · Acceptance checklist

Build against this, not against the pictures. Each line is a pass/fail a second person can run.

✓SurfaceTest
☐Contrast orderDesaturate a frame with a wave running. Order brightest-last is field, nebula, grid, route, pulsar, station, projectile, contact, hit flash. Any inversion fails.
☐Route directionA player shown a still frame, with no chevron visible, can say which way contacts travel.
☐Stream motion budgetAt 4× on Switchback, a single Mote is the first thing the eye finds. Also: the board layer still does no tessellation per frame.
☐NebulaNo tile boundary is traceable in the cloud. Blocked tiles are still individually identifiable as unbuildable.
☐Three fieldsSame Lance, same blue; same Drifter, same pink — on all three boards. No field contains a warm value above the nebula's brightness.
☐Slot statesAvailable, armed, can't-afford and locked are distinguishable with the type hidden. Can't-afford and locked never look alike.
☐Glyph learningA player who has built a station once can pick its slot out of five by the glyph alone.
☐Inspector word countTen words or fewer in the selected state, including the sell button. Every figure still sourced from nextStats.
☐Collar legendA player shown an upgraded station on the board can name which path was bought, having only ever seen the inspector.
☐Deck toggleTab does not make the board jump distractingly, and the closed deck triggers no DOM writes on kills.
☐Ribbon dangerAt 4 lives the alarm is noticed within one second from a board-focused gaze — and the fill still reads as progress, not as a second quantity.
☐Status channelsOne ring per contact, ever. Six slowed contacts in a file are countable. A charged Filament is distinguishable from a cold one at a glance.

Deliberately not in this spec

  • Race HUD and results screen. Undesigned, and they should inherit this vocabulary rather than invent beside it. Next document.
  • Audio. Several of the entries above ("regen start is silent", "a Cluster death looks like any death") are cheaper to solve with sound than with pixels. Worth a pass once the visual channels are settled.
  • Balance. Nothing here moves a number. Every figure shown is live from src/content/.
